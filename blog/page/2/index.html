    Paper vs hardware 10: Robometer

    Robometer (Liang, Korkmaz, Zhang, Hwang et al.; arXiv 2603.02115) is a 4B reward model trained on a million trajectories. I tested it zero-shot as a judge on my own rollouts and on a 2 099-rollout public SO-101 benchmark. Episode length beat it on every held-out task. The paper never computes episode length.

    Paper vs hardware 12: TurboVLA

    TurboVLA (Xie, Yao, Wu et al.; arXiv 2607.27205) drops the language model from the VLA and binds vision to text with Grounding-DINO-style cross-attention. My port failed on a two-task dataset, I concluded it ignored the instruction, then I found my port differed from the paper in four places, then I ran the paper's own trainer on my data. The faithful model ignores the instruction too. The data was the whole story.

    Paper vs hardware 9: VLA-Corrector

    VLA-Corrector (Song, Qian, Feng, Song; arXiv 2609.06508) wraps a frozen π0.5 in a tiny learned verifier that routes a recovery prompt. I borrowed one of its signals — cosine drift of the latent — as a monitor. It caught nothing. The part of the paper I should have borrowed is the verifier's design.

    Paper vs hardware 13: VLA-JEPA

    VLA-JEPA (Sun, Zhang, Qi et al.; arXiv 2602.10098) pretrains a VLA on human video through a latent world model. I trained it for $10, ran it on the arm at 9/10, and tested whether its eight samples were worth choosing between. The paper's honest ablation row says what to expect: robustness, not accuracy.

    v1.5: the station as a harness for a coding agent

    Three weeks of gates reduced the world-model stack to a verifier and a shadow signal. What remained was the actual bottleneck of a one-person robotics lab: deciding what to record next, running trials unattended, and keeping the training data clean. v1.5 is that loop, specified from nine recent papers on agent harnesses and self-improvement, and partly built. It has not run end to end on the arm yet; this post is a design note, not a result.

    Two gates I voided, and the two-line check that would have caught them

    On 2026-10-18 two Jetson gates went into the register with bars frozen beforehand: G11-A passed, G12 failed. Both were voided the same day. G11-A timed the right model with the wrong weights; G12 timed the wrong model. Neither was a training or hardware problem. Both were caught by checking what the script had actually loaded.

    Verify at rest: six mid-episode monitors fail in one day

    One Jetson day, no arm motion, every mid-episode verifier in the v1.0 design run against 50 fresh rollouts with the bars frozen the night before. Stage trackers, VLM judges and the world-model reflex all failed. One offline engine experiment passed. The architecture that came out of it (v1.1) verifies only at rest, and that rule has not moved since.
